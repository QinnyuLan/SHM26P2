# Geometry updates through a frozen semantic readout: prior-art boundary

2026-09-27. This note records a limited primary-source check, not a novelty guarantee. The proposed full-versus-prior-only experiment has no measured outcome yet. Ordinary scene-level CE, stopping gradients and the chain-rule decomposition are not new algorithms.

| Primary source | Existing mechanism and limit of the comparison |
|---|---|
| [High-level Feature Guided Decoding for Semantic Segmentation (HFGD)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08646) | Uses stop-gradient to protect high-level semantic features from gradients of a low-level upsampling/refinement path. Therefore protecting a representation from its decoder's backward route is established. It does not establish that the context route through this project's fixed H3 head harms shared Gaussian means. |
| [DeGenseGS (2026), §2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04761v1) | Separates semantic evolution and geometric deformation using property-specific decoding branches, motivated by semantic changes causing spurious surgical deformation. Semantic/geometric gradient decoupling is established. Its dynamic surgical representation is different from keeping one static Gaussian field, its categorical q, attributes and head fixed while changing only means. That distinction alone does not establish novelty. |
| [Two-View Accumulation / CrossGrad-GS (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00052v1) | Examines gradient reconciliation in Gaussian splatting against matched-compute controls; its abstract and reported comparisons identify two-view accumulation as the dominant lever, with several reconciliation variants within seed variation of random pairing. Thus a new gradient split, negative cosine, or benefit obtained with more rendered views is insufficient evidence for a distinct mechanism. |

For the actual H3 readout, let `p(means)` be its rendered categorical prior and `e(means)` its features, RGB, depth, alpha and moments. The same prediction is `softmax(log p + refiner(p,e))`. A prior-only derivative keeps **all** p dependencies, including the head's entropy input, while stopping all e inputs. The complete derivative is the sum of prior and context contributions; their separation does not itself prescribe a better update. Adam, FP32 realization and the geometric cap can further change the effect of these gradients.

The remaining empirical question is narrow: does allowing the context route to move the same shared Gaussian means help or hinder the deployed frozen readout compared with moving means through the categorical prior route? Raw improvement accompanied by scene/joint regression motivates examining the question, but does not identify its cause. The previous fixed-geometry feature/nullspace diagnostic also does not answer it: it changed the differentiation variable and held the renderer weights fixed, whereas means movement changes visibility and the mixing of every rendered attribute.

Any useful contribution must therefore come from a reproducible result under identical forward predictions, data exposure, VJP/render budget, initialization and optimizer settings, with actual displacement magnitudes and complete scene performance reported. A full/prior arm difference alone need not isolate a unique causal mechanism; displacement magnitude, approximation and optimization effects remain alternatives. A negative result closes the fixed routing experiment, not all possible geometric refinement. No current result supports a first-method or performance-superiority claim.
